### STORY LENGTH & DETAIL CALIBRATION

**Target total rounds:** {{TOTAL_MESSAGES_TARGET}}

**Expected scene count:** {{EXPECTED_SCENE_COUNT}}

**Scene composition:**
- **situation**: 1–2 sentences setting the scene
- **key_events_if_unchallenged**: 3–5 distinct events/beats (not exhaustive; player choices will add more)
- **choice_points**: 0–2 per scene (focus only on story-altering decisions, not micro-actions)
- **character_focus**: 1–3 entries naming characters and their emotional beat or role in the scene
- **hooks_for_future**: 2–4 threads connecting to later scenes, antagonistic forces, or character arcs
- **beats**: 3–7 beats per scene that advance the scene through its purpose (see Beat Types below)
- **expected_rounds**: (optional) Specify how many rounds this scene should span. If omitted, scenes will be paced based on story length.

Aim to keep your scene composition to 1000 words or less.

**Beat Types (use for scene beats):**
Each scene should have 3-7 beats that mark key moments of progression. Use these beat types:

- **establishment**: Set the scene, location, mood, or character state at the start
- **hook**: Create interest with a question, mystery, tension, or revelation
- **reaction**: Show character responses through dialogue, internal thought, or action
- **escalation**: Raise stakes with a complication, revelation, or conflict
- **pivot**: Change direction with a twist, decision, discovery, or reversal
- **emotional**: Hit a specific emotional note (joy, fear, connection, loss, etc.)
- **transition**: Bridge to next scene with cliffhanger, setup, or reflection

**Beat Guidelines:**
- Start scenes with establishment and hook beats
- Include 1-2 emotional beats to ground character experiences
- Use escalation or pivot beats to raise tension
- End scenes with transition beats that set up the next scene
- Mark critical beats as `required: true` (essential to scene completion)
- Mark optional beats as `required: false` (can be skipped or condensed)
- Provide specific `pacing_constraints` for beats that need special handling (e.g., "This beat should unfold gradually over 2-3 rounds")

### IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **Player agency is paramount.** The blueprint guides but never railroads. Every scene includes potential choice points where player actions matter.

2. **Detail for runtime utility.** The LLM narrator uses scene_plan excerpts each turn. Provide enough specificity (situation, character focus, emotional beats) that the narrator can adapt smoothly without losing coherence.

3. **Respect the metaphor level.** Don't accidentally slip into metaphor when literal is requested, or vice versa. The genre_realism_notes field should accurately reflect what you implemented.

4. **Character consistency.** Use exact names and respect stated personalities. Character arcs should feel earned, not imposed.

5. **Thematic cohesion.** Scenes, antagonistic forces, and resolutions should align thematically with the story type and author style (if provided).

6. **Flexibility in structure.** While scene_plan is sequential, note that real play may skip, merge, or reorder scenes. Provide enough hooks and interconnections that the blueprint works even if scenes are shuffled.

7. **Choice points are strategic.** Don't include trivial micro-choices ("Do you go left or right?"). Focus on decisions that alter plot, character relationships, or thematic direction.

8. **Tone consistency.** Maintain the specified tone (or story type's default tone) across all descriptions, scene titles, character arc language, and style notes.
