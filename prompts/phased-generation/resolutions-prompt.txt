## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **The primary ending is the definitive conclusion.** You must select ONE ending as the primary (most story-appropriate) ending based on:
   - Character arc completion established in Phase 2
   - Thematic coherence with the story's central ideas
   - Narrative flow from the scenes designed in Phase 3
   - Emotional resonance that feels earned and satisfying

2. **Primary ending selection criteria:**
   - **Character arcs complete**: The ending should resolve or deliberately transform the key character journeys established in Phase 2
   - **Thematic payoff**: The ending should deliver on the story's central themes, providing a clear statement about what the story ultimately "says"
   - **Narrative inevitability**: Based on the scene plan from Phase 3, the ending should feel like the natural culmination of events
   - **Emotional satisfaction**: The ending should provide emotional closure appropriate to the story's tone (tragic stories end tragically, hopeful stories end with hope, etc.)

3. **Alternate endings provide flexibility.** Generate exactly 2 alternate endings that could work if story developments diverge from the expected path. These should:
   - Explore different thematic angles or character choices
   - Feel logically consistent with the established story
   - Differ meaningfully from the primary ending (not just trivial variations)

4. **Character consistency is paramount.** All character outcomes (in both primary and alternate endings) must align with their arcs from Phase 2. Endings should feel like the logical culmination (or deliberate subversion) of established character journeys.

5. **Thematic cohesion.** Each ending should explore different aspects of the story's central themes. The primary ending should provide the most thematically coherent resolution, while alternates can explore variations.

6. **Respect the story's tone.** If the story is dark, the primary ending shouldn't be unrealistically optimistic. If the story is hopeful, the primary ending should provide closure that matches that tone.

7. **Consequences matter.** Show how the scene plan from Phase 3 leads to these outcomes. Each ending should feel connected to the scenes you've designed.

8. **Ambiguity as a tool.** The primary ending can leave certain questions unanswered, as long as the emotional arc feels complete. Not everything needs to be wrapped up neatly.
