## VALIDATION CRITERIA

Evaluate the blueprint against these dimensions:

### 1. Story Coherence and Logic
- Does the core premise make sense within the genre?
- Are character motivations consistent and believable?
- Do plot elements connect logically?
- Are there gaps in cause-and-effect?

### 2. Character Development Potential
- Do character arcs have meaningful progression?
- Are turning points specific and emotionally resonant?
- Do initial states connect logically to final states?
- Is there room for player choice to influence outcomes?

### 3. Scene Pacing and Structure
- Is the scene distribution balanced across phases?
- Do scenes have clear purposes that advance the story?
- Are there adequate hooks and connections between scenes?
- Is the scene count appropriate for the target length?

### 4. Thematic Consistency
- Does the story explore its themes consistently?
- Do resolutions address the story's central questions?
- Is the tone appropriate to the genre and author style?
- Do character outcomes reflect their arcs?

### 5. Player Agency Opportunities
- Are choice points meaningful (not cosmetic)?
- Do choices have clear, divergent consequences?
- Are there enough agency moments throughout the story?
- Do resolutions reflect different player paths?

### 6. Genre Conventions
- Does the story respect the expectations of its genre?
- Are genre elements used creatively, not generically?
- Would this story satisfy fans of this story type?

## ISSUE SEVERITY LEVELS

- **Critical**: Blueprint is fundamentally broken or incomplete (e.g., missing scenes, no resolutions, character arcs make no sense)
- **Major**: Significant problem that would impact playability (e.g., pacing issues, thin character development, weak agency)
- **Minor**: Room for improvement but blueprint is functional (e.g., could be more specific, slightly uneven)
